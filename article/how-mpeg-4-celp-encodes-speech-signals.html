How MPEG-4 CELP Encodes Speech Signals

MPEG-4 Code Excited Linear Prediction (CELP) encodes speech signals by modeling the physical human vocal mechanism to achieve high-quality audio at low bitrates. It separates the speech production process into two primary components: a Linear Prediction (LP) synthesis filter that represents the short-term spectral envelope of the vocal tract, and an excitation signal derived from adaptive and fixed codebooks that represents the vocal cord vibrations. Through an analysis-by-synthesis optimization loop, the encoder determines the optimal filter coefficients and codebook vectors that produce a synthesized sound closest to the original speech.

Linear Prediction Analysis

The encoding process begins by dividing the input speech signal into short frames, typically 10 to 30 milliseconds in length, where speech characteristics remain relatively stationary.

  1. Calculating LPC Coefficients: The encoder performs Linear Predictive Coding (LPC) analysis on each frame using methods such as the autocorrelation method and the Levinson-Durbin algorithm. This calculates the short-term correlation between adjacent audio samples, producing Linear Prediction coefficients that model the resonances (formants) of the vocal tract.
  2. Quantization and Stability: To ensure filter stability during transmission and interpolation, the LPC coefficients are transformed into Line Spectral Pairs (LSP) or Line Spectral Frequencies (LSF). These representations are vector-quantized and transmitted to the decoder.

Excitation Signal Decomposition

The excitation signal that drives the LP synthesis filter is modeled as the combination of two distinct codebooks: the adaptive codebook and the fixed (stochastic) codebook.

1. Adaptive Codebook (Pitch Modeling)

The adaptive codebook models the long-term periodicity of speech, such as voiced vowels produced by vibrating vocal cords.

  • It consists of delayed versions of previously generated excitation signals.
  • The encoder searches for a pitch delay (lag) and an associated pitch gain that best predict the harmonic structure of the current subframe.

2. Fixed Algebraic Codebook (Residual Modeling)

After removing the periodic component via the adaptive codebook, a residual error signal remains. The fixed codebook models this non-periodic or noise-like component, which is crucial for unvoiced consonants and transient sounds.

  • MPEG-4 CELP typically uses an Algebraic CELP (ACELP) structure.
  • Instead of storing vast lookup tables, the algebraic codebook uses a mathematical framework of a few interleaved pulse positions with predetermined amplitudes (+1 or -1).
  • This allows the encoder to rapidly compute and represent complex residual waveforms with minimal bit overhead.

Analysis-by-Synthesis Optimization

MPEG-4 CELP uses a closed-loop "analysis-by-synthesis" (AbS) method to select the best excitation vectors:

  1. Candidate Synthesis: The encoder combines candidate vectors from the adaptive and fixed codebooks, scales them by their respective gain parameters, and passes the resulting excitation through the LP synthesis filter to generate candidate speech waveforms.
  2. Perceptual Weighting: The generated speech and the original speech are passed through a perceptual weighting filter. This filter reshapes the error spectrum according to human psychoacoustic masking properties, ensuring that quantization noise is concentrated in frequency regions where the speech energy is high and human hearing is least sensitive.
  3. Minimizing Mean Squared Error (MSE): The encoder calculates the perceptually weighted error between the synthesized speech and the original speech. It systematically searches the codebook parameters to find the combination of pitch lag, algebraic pulse positions, and gain factors that minimizes this error.

Bitstream Generation

Once the optimal parameters are found for each frame and its constituent subframes, they are packed into the bitstream. The transmitted data consists of:

  • Quantized LSP/LSF indices (vocal tract filter shape)
  • Pitch lag and adaptive codebook gain (pitch and periodicity)
  • Algebraic codebook pulse positions, signs, and fixed codebook gain (residual excitation)

The decoder unpacks these parameters, reconstructs the excitation signal, and runs it through the LP synthesis filter to reconstruct the speech signal.
