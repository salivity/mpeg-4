MPEG-4 Tools to Detect and Remove 3:2 Pulldown

Telecine 3:2 pulldown is a telecine conversion process that transfers 24-frame-per-second film material to 29.97-frame-per-second (60 Hz) interlaced video formats by duplicating alternating fields. MPEG-4 standards address this issue through a combination of syntax-level metadata flags, picture-level coding structures, and encoder pre-processing algorithms. By utilizing syntax elements such as repeat field flags, Supplemental Enhancement Information (SEI) messages, and adaptive frame/field coding, MPEG-4 enables decoders to reconstruct pure progressive frames without encoding redundant interlaced fields or producing comb artifacts.

Picture-Level Header Flags: repeat_first_field and top_field_first

In MPEG-4 Part 2 (Visual) and Part 10 (AVC/H.264), the primary mechanism for eliminating 3:2 pulldown artifacts is progressive frame coding paired with display signaling flags. Rather than encoding duplicate fields, the encoder encodes the source as native 24p progressive frames and sets two specific syntax elements in the picture header:

  • repeat_first_field (RFF): Instructs the decoder to repeat the first displayed field for an additional field duration, turning a two-field frame into a three-field sequence at display time.
  • top_field_first (TFF): Dictates whether the top or bottom field is scanned first to maintain field parity.

By alternating these flags across consecutive frames, the video stream preserves standard 3:2 cadence on interlaced displays without wasting compression bitrate on duplicate data or baking interlacing artifacts into the compressed stream.

Picture Timing SEI Messages and pic_struct (MPEG-4 AVC)

MPEG-4 Part 10 (AVC/H.264) introduces advanced Supplemental Enhancement Information (SEI) messages to handle pulldown elimination and display cadence. The Picture Timing SEI message contains the pic_struct syntax element, which defines the precise temporal layout of a frame. Standard values include:

  • pic_struct = 5: Frame displayed as Top, Bottom, then Top field repeated.
  • pic_struct = 6: Frame displayed as Bottom, Top, then Bottom field repeated.
  • pic_struct = 7 and 8: Frame doubling and frame tripling for progressive displays.

This explicit structure allows decoders to recognize telecined film content immediately and render it progressively via Inverse Telecine (IVTC) without requiring real-time heuristic field matching.

Macroblock-Adaptive Frame/Field (MBAFF) and PAFF Coding

When dealing with video that contains mixed progressive and hard-telecined interlaced material, MPEG-4 uses Picture-Adaptive Field/Frame (PAFF) and Macroblock-Adaptive Frame/Field (MBAFF) coding:

  • PAFF: Allows the encoder to toggle between frame-based and field-based encoding at the picture level, isolating telecined sequences from pure progressive sequences.
  • MBAFF: Operates at the \(16 \times 16\) macroblock level, allowing macroblock pairs in moving, telecined regions to be encoded as individual fields while non-moving regions remain progressive frames. This prevents cross-field comb distortion from being permanently blended into motion-compensated reference blocks.

Pre-Processing Cadence Detection and Inverse Telecine (IVTC)

Before syntax encoding, MPEG-4 compliant encoders use integrated IVTC modules to detect the 3:2 pattern:

  • Sum of Absolute Differences (SAD) Field Matching: The encoder compares adjacent fields by calculating pixel difference metrics across consecutive fields. A 3:2 pulldown cadence exhibits a repeating pattern of identical or near-identical fields every five field intervals.
  • Comb Artifact Filtering: Vertical difference filtering identifies inter-field combing caused by motion in telecined frames.
  • Cadence Lock and Broken Cadence Recovery: The encoder locks onto the regular 3:2 sequence. If an edit point disrupts the cadence (a "bad edit"), the cadence tracker drops back to field-based processing or resets the sequence to avoid frame blending errors. Once matched, redundant fields are dropped, and the stream is compressed as a clean 23.976p sequence.
