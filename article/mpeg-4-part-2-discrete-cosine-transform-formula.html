MPEG-4 Part 2 Discrete Cosine Transform Formula

This article outlines the mathematical formulation of the two-dimensional Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT) and Inverse Discrete Cosine Transform (IDCT) used in the MPEG-4 Part 2 (Visual) compression standard. It breaks down the forward transform equation, the inverse reconstruction equation, scaling factors, and key properties governing \(8 \times 8\) pixel block processing.

The 2D Forward Discrete Cosine Transform (FDCT)

MPEG-4 Part 2 divides image frames and residual prediction error blocks into \(8 \times 8\) arrays of samples. The forward DCT transforms an \(8 \times 8\) spatial domain block of samples \(f(x, y)\) into an \(8 \times 8\) frequency domain coefficient matrix \(F(u, v)\).

The mathematical formulation for the forward 2D DCT is:

\[F(u, v) = \frac{1}{4} C(u) C(v) \sum_{x=0}^{7} \sum_{y=0}^{7} f(x, y) \cos\left[ \frac{(2x+1)u\pi}{16} \right] \cos\left[ \frac{(2y+1)v\pi}{16} \right]\]

Where:

  • \(x\) and \(y\) are the spatial coordinates within the block (\(x, y \in \{0, 1, \dots, 7\}\)).
  • \(u\) and \(v\) are the horizontal and vertical spatial frequency indices (\(u, v \in \{0, 1, \dots, 7\}\)).
  • \(f(x, y)\) is the input pixel value or motion-compensated residual value at position \((x, y)\).
  • \(F(u, v)\) is the resulting DCT coefficient at frequency coordinates \((u, v)\).

Normalization Factors

The scaling factors \(C(u)\) and \(C(v)\) ensure the transform remains orthonormal:

\[C(u) = \begin{cases} \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}, & \text{for } u = 0 \\ 1, & \text{for } u > 0 \end{cases}\]

\[C(v) = \begin{cases} \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}, & \text{for } v = 0 \\ 1, & \text{for } v > 0 \end{cases}\]

When \(u = 0\) and \(v = 0\), \(F(0,0)\) represents the DC coefficient (the average energy of the block). All other combinations of \((u, v)\) yield the AC coefficients, representing progressively higher spatial frequencies.

The 2D Inverse Discrete Cosine Transform (IDCT)

To reconstruct the spatial domain samples from the quantized frequency coefficients, MPEG-4 Part 2 uses the 2D Inverse DCT:

\[f(x, y) = \frac{1}{4} \sum_{u=0}^{7} \sum_{v=0}^{7} C(u) C(v) F(u, v) \cos\left[ \frac{(2x+1)u\pi}{16} \right] \cos\left[ \frac{(2y+1)v\pi}{16} \right]\]

The variables and scaling factors \(C(u)\) and \(C(v)\) retain the same definitions as in the forward transform.

Mathematical Properties in MPEG-4 Part 2

  • Separability: The 2D DCT kernel is separable into two successive 1D transforms. In practice, the 2D DCT is computed by applying an \(8\)-point 1D DCT along the rows of the block, followed by an \(8\)-point 1D DCT along the columns: \[F(u, v) = \text{DCT}_{1D\_col}\left( \text{DCT}_{1D\_row}\left( f(x, y) \right) \right)\]
  • Input Preprocessing: For intra-coded macroblocks with 8-bit unsigned pixel values (\(0\) to \(255\)), input samples are level-shifted by subtracting \(128\) prior to the forward DCT to center values around zero.
  • Accuracy and Drift Control: Because floating-point operations can cause rounding differences across different decoders, MPEG-4 Part 2 strictly defines IDCT numerical precision and mismatch control rules (such as clipping reconstructed values to the \([-256, 255]\) range and limiting cumulative rounding errors) to prevent IDCT drift during inter-frame reconstruction.
