MDCT Filterbanks in MPEG-4 AAC Explained

The MPEG-4 Advanced Audio Coding (AAC) standard relies on the Modified Discrete Cosine Transform (MDCT) filterbank as its core time-to-frequency transformation engine. By converting continuous time-domain audio signals into discrete frequency-domain subbands, the MDCT enables AAC to apply sophisticated psychoacoustic models, eliminate redundant data, and achieve high-fidelity audio compression. This article explains the technical mechanics of the MDCT filterbank in MPEG-4 AAC, including overlapping windowing, dynamic block switching, and its role in perceptual coding.

The Role of MDCT in AAC Compression

MPEG-4 AAC is a transform-based perceptual audio codec. Before any psychoacoustic compression or quantization can occur, the input audio waveform must be decomposed into its constituent spectral components. AAC employs an MDCT filterbank for this operation because it provides high energy compaction, critical sampling, and robust frequency selectivity, making it far superior to standard Discrete Cosine Transforms (DCT) or Discrete Fourier Transforms (DFT) for audio signals.

Overlapping and Time-Domain Aliasing Cancellation (TDAC)

A standard block transform introduces severe discontinuities at block boundaries, heard as audible clicking or blocking artifacts. The MDCT solves this through a 50% overlap-add architecture coupled with Time-Domain Aliasing Cancellation (TDAC).

  1. 50% Overlap: Each transform window overlaps the previous window by half its length.
  2. Aliasing Introduction and Cancellation: The forward MDCT deliberately introduces time-domain aliasing into the transform coefficients to maintain critical sampling without increasing the data rate. When the inverse MDCT (IMDCT) is computed at the decoder, this aliasing is mirrored symmetrically.
  3. Overlap-Add Reconstruction: Adding the overlapping halves of adjacent reconstructed frames perfectly cancels out the aliasing components, yielding a seamless, artifact-free output signal.

Dynamic Block Switching

Audio signals constantly vary between stationary tones (like sustained strings) and sharp transients (like drum hits). To handle both efficiently, MPEG-4 AAC dynamically alternates between two primary MDCT window sizes:

  • Long Blocks (2048 samples): Yields 1024 spectral coefficients. Long windows provide high frequency resolution, allowing the psychoacoustic model to identify closely spaced tonal components and maximize coding efficiency during stationary audio passages.
  • Short Blocks (256 samples): Yields 128 spectral coefficients, processed in sequences of eight sub-blocks to cover the same time span as a long block. Short windows provide high temporal resolution, preventing "pre-echo" artifacts where quantization noise from a sharp transient smears backward in time.
  • Transition Blocks (Long-to-Short and Short-to-Long): Asymmetric transition windows ensure smooth mathematical handoffs between long and short window modes without breaking the TDAC condition.

Window Shape Selection

MPEG-4 AAC enhances the MDCT filterbank by supporting two distinct window shapes:

  • Sine Window: The default window, offering excellent frequency selectivity and energy separation for general audio signals and harmonic content.
  • Kaiser-Bessel Derived (KBD) Window: A specialized window with adjustable stopband attenuation, used to provide better rejection of out-of-band noise for complex signals with strong transient behavior.

AAC can switch between Sine and KBD windows on a frame-by-frame basis to optimize the transform for the specific characteristics of the incoming audio.

Integration with Psychoacoustic Quantization

Once the MDCT converts the time-domain signal into spectral coefficients, AAC groups these coefficients into scalefactor bands that emulate the human ear's critical bands (Bark scale). The psychoacoustic model evaluates the signal's masking thresholds across these bands, determining the maximum allowable quantization distortion that remains completely inaudible to human listeners. Quantization and Huffman coding are then applied directly to the MDCT spectral values, achieving substantial file size reduction while preserving perceptual audio quality.
