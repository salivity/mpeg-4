MPEG-4 AVC 4x4 Intra-Prediction Modes Explained

In MPEG-4 AVC (also known as H.264), intra-prediction reduces spatial redundancy within video frames by predicting current block samples from adjacent, previously decoded boundary pixels. For high-detail areas, the standard provides nine specific intra-prediction modes for 4x4 luma blocks, consisting of one DC (average) mode and eight directional modes.

These modes utilize up to 13 neighboring boundary pixels—specifically four above-left, four directly above, four to the above-right, and four to the left—to generate a 4x4 prediction block. The encoder evaluates the efficiency of each mode and signals the optimal choice in the bitstream.

Mode 0: Vertical

The four samples directly above the current 4x4 block are copied vertically downward to form each column in the block. This mode is ideal for areas with vertical structures or textures.

Mode 1: Horizontal

The four samples directly to the left of the current block are copied horizontally to the right to form each row in the block. This mode is optimal for horizontal edges.

Mode 2: DC (Direct Current)

The sample values are generated by computing the arithmetic mean of all available upper and left neighboring pixels. If only the upper or left samples are available (such as at frame boundaries), the prediction uses only the available set. If neither is available, a default value (typically 128 for 8-bit video) is used.

Mode 3: Diagonal Down-Left

Samples are interpolated diagonally from the top-right to the bottom-left at an angle of 45 degrees. It uses weighted averages of the pixels located above and above-right of the current block.

Mode 4: Diagonal Down-Right

Samples are interpolated diagonally from the top-left to the bottom-right at a 45-degree angle. This mode relies on the corner pixel (above-left) as well as the immediate left and top neighboring pixels.

Mode 5: Vertical-Right

Samples are interpolated at an angle of approximately 26.6 degrees to the right of the vertical axis. It blends samples from the top and top-left borders.

Mode 6: Horizontal-Down

Samples are interpolated at an angle of approximately 26.6 degrees below the horizontal axis, projecting downwards and to the right using the left and top-left border pixels.

Mode 7: Vertical-Left

Samples are interpolated at an angle of approximately 26.6 degrees to the left of the vertical axis, projecting down and left using the top and top-right neighboring pixels.

Mode 8: Horizontal-Up

Samples are interpolated at an angle of approximately 26.6 degrees above the horizontal axis, projecting upwards and to the right using only the pixels along the left boundary.
