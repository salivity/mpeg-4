MPEG-4 Chroma Intra-Prediction Across Color Formats

This article examines how MPEG-4—specifically MPEG-4 Part 10 (AVC/H.264) and its Fidelity Range Extensions (FRExt)—handles chroma intra-prediction across 4:2:0, 4:2:2, and 4:4:4 color sampling structures. It details how the codec dynamically adapts block sizes, prediction directions, and mathematical filtering to maintain spatial redundancy reduction regardless of chroma resolution.

Fundamentals of Chroma Intra-Prediction

Intra-prediction reduces spatial redundancy by predicting the pixel values of a target block using adjacent, previously decoded boundary samples from the top and left neighboring blocks. While early MPEG-4 standards (Part 2) relied primarily on transform-domain DC and AC coefficient prediction, modern MPEG-4 implementations (Part 10 / AVC) perform spatial domain intra-prediction for both luma and chroma components.

Because different chroma sampling structures alter the spatial dimensions of the chroma planes relative to the luma plane (16x16 macroblocks), the prediction mechanisms adapt accordingly.

4:2:0 Chroma Sampling

In the standard 4:2:0 structure, chroma channels (\(Cb\) and \(Cr\)) are subsampled by half horizontally and vertically, resulting in an 8x8 pixel block per macroblock.

  • Block Size: Intra-prediction operates on an 8x8 chroma block.
  • Available Modes: Four spatial prediction modes are used:
    • DC Mode (Mode 0): Averages the available top and left neighbor pixels to produce a flat prediction block.
    • Horizontal Mode (Mode 1): Extends the left reconstructed column horizontally across the block.
    • Vertical Mode (Mode 2): Extends the top reconstructed row vertically down the block.
    • Plane Mode (Mode 3): Applies a linear spatial interpolation across the horizontal and vertical directions to model smooth gradients.
  • Shared Prediction: Both \(Cb\) and \(Cr\) channels utilize the same intra-prediction mode selection within a single macroblock to save signaling overhead.

4:2:2 Chroma Sampling

In 4:2:2 sampling, chroma is subsampled by half horizontally but retains full vertical resolution, yielding an 8x16 chroma block per macroblock.

  • Block Size: Prediction operates on 8x16 rectangular blocks.
  • Algorithm Adaptation: The same four basic modes (DC, Horizontal, Vertical, Plane) are employed, but their mathematical formulations adapt to the non-square geometry:
    • DC Mode: Re-weights the average calculation based on 8 top samples and 16 left samples.
    • Vertical & Horizontal Modes: The top 8 samples are extended downward across 16 rows, while the left 16 samples are projected horizontally across 8 columns.
    • Plane Mode: The gradient equations and slope calculations are adjusted to account for the asymmetric horizontal (\(H=8\)) and vertical (\(V=16\)) boundary dimensions.

4:4:4 Chroma Sampling

In full-resolution 4:4:4 sampling, chroma planes have the exact same spatial dimensions as the luma plane (16x16 pixels per macroblock). MPEG-4 handles 4:4:4 chroma intra-prediction using two distinct methods:

  1. Integrated 4:4:4 Mode:

    • Uses 16x16 chroma blocks employing the standard four chroma modes (DC, Horizontal, Vertical, Plane), or:
    • Employs the full suite of luma intra-prediction modes (such as 4x4, 8x8, and 16x16 directional modes, offering up to 9 distinct angles/directions) directly on the chroma channels.

  2. Separate Color Plane Coding:

    • Splits the color video into three completely independent monochrome channels (Y, Cb, Cr or R, G, B).
    • Each channel is encoded independently as a separate luma-like plane, allowing each color component to independently select its own partition sizes (4x4, 8x8, 16x16) and all 9 directional prediction modes without being tied to the primary luma channel decisions.

Boundary Handling and Filtering

Across all sampling structures, boundary samples undergo specific availability checks:

  • If upper or left blocks are missing (e.g., at frame edges or slice boundaries), the prediction fallback logic adjusts dynamically, substituting available boundaries or defaulting to fixed mid-level values (e.g., 128 for 8-bit video).
  • For larger partition sizes in high-profile 4:4:4 workflows, low-pass smoothing filters are applied to boundary reference samples prior to directional extrapolation to prevent high-frequency prediction artifacts.
