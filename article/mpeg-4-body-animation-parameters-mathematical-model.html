MPEG 4 Body Animation Parameters Mathematical Model

This article provides a technical overview of the mathematical formulation underpinning Body Animation Parameters (BAPs) within the MPEG-4 Synthetic/Natural Hybrid Coding (SNHC) standard. It details the hierarchical kinematic framework, local joint transformation equations, rotational parameterization methods, normalization mechanisms based on Body Definition Parameters (BDPs), and the linear blend skinning formulas used to deform synthetic body meshes.

Kinematic Hierarchy and Forward Kinematics

The MPEG-4 Body Animation architecture models the human body as an articulated kinematic chain composed of rigid segments connected by joints. The spatial configuration of any segment \(i\) is calculated recursively relative to its parent joint in the hierarchy using Forward Kinematics (FK).

The global transformation matrix \(\mathbf{M}_i\) for joint \(i\) is given by:

\[\mathbf{M}_i = \mathbf{M}_{\text{parent}(i)} \cdot \mathbf{T}_{i, \text{bind}} \cdot \mathbf{R}_i(\text{BAP}) \cdot \mathbf{T}_i(\text{BAP})\]

Where:

  • \(\mathbf{M}_{\text{parent}(i)}\) is the \(4 \times 4\) homogeneous global transformation matrix of the parent joint.
  • \(\mathbf{T}_{i, \text{bind}}\) is the static translation matrix defining the joint offset in the default reference posture (neutral pose).
  • \(\mathbf{R}_i(\text{BAP})\) is the time-varying rotation matrix driven by rotational BAPs.
  • \(\mathbf{T}_i(\text{BAP})\) is the translational matrix driven by translation BAPs (primarily applied to the root joint/pelvis).

Rotational Parameterization

MPEG-4 defines up to 296 BAPs, covering rotational degrees of freedom (DOFs) across standard anatomical joints. Joint rotations are parameterized using Euler angles executed in a standardized sequence (typically \(Z\)-\(X\)-\(Y\) or \(X\)-\(Y\)-\(Z\) depending on the joint specification):

\[\mathbf{R}_i = \mathbf{R}_z(\theta_z) \cdot \mathbf{R}_x(\theta_x) \cdot \mathbf{R}_y(\theta_y)\]

For an individual rotation about an axis \(\mathbf{u} = [u_x, u_y, u_z]^T\) by an angle \(\theta\), the rotation matrix \(\mathbf{R}(\theta, \mathbf{u})\) corresponds to Rodrigues' rotation formula:

\[\mathbf{R}(\theta, \mathbf{u}) = \mathbf{I} + (\sin\theta)\mathbf{K} + (1 - \cos\theta)\mathbf{K}^2\]

Where \(\mathbf{I}\) is the \(3 \times 3\) identity matrix and \(\mathbf{K}\) is the skew-symmetric cross-product matrix of the unit vector \(\mathbf{u}\):

\[\mathbf{K} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & -u_z & u_y \\ u_z & 0 & -u_x \\ -u_y & u_x & 0 \end{bmatrix}\]

Quantization and Normalization Mechanics

BAP streams transmit integer values to optimize bandwidth. To convert transmitted integer values into continuous physical angles or translations, a dequantization and scaling formula is applied:

\[\theta = \text{BAP\_Value} \times s_{\text{angular}}\]

For rotational parameters, \(s_{\text{angular}}\) is defined in thousandths of a radian (\(10^{-3}\text{ rad}\)) or fractions of degrees.

For translational parameters, normalization relies on Body Definition Parameters (BDPs) to ensure model independence. Distances are scaled using standard body dimension units (such as the distance between hips or head-to-neck height):

\[\mathbf{t} = \text{BAP\_Value} \cdot s_{\text{trans}} \cdot D_{\text{ref}}\]

Where \(D_{\text{ref}}\) is a reference dimension scalar extracted from the model's BDP set.

Mesh Surface Deformation (Linear Blend Skinning)

Once the global transformation matrices \(\mathbf{M}_i\) are updated by the BAP stream, the synthetic character's skin vertices are deformed using Linear Blend Skinning (LBS). The deformed position \(\mathbf{v}'\) of a vertex \(\mathbf{v}_0\) in rest pose is computed as:

\[\mathbf{v}' = \sum_{j \in J} w_j \cdot \mathbf{M}_j \cdot \mathbf{M}_{j, 0}^{-1} \cdot \mathbf{v}_0\]

Subject to the partition of unity constraint:

\[\sum_{j \in J} w_j = 1, \quad w_j \ge 0\]

Where:

  • \(J\) is the set of influencing joints for the vertex.
  • \(w_j\) is the scalar skinning weight linking the vertex to joint \(j\).
  • \(\mathbf{M}_j\) is the current global transformation matrix of joint \(j\).
  • \(\mathbf{M}_{j, 0}^{-1}\) is the inverse rest-pose transformation matrix of joint \(j\).
