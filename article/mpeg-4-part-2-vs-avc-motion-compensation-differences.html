MPEG-4 Part 2 vs AVC Motion Compensation Differences

Motion compensation is a fundamental video compression technique designed to reduce temporal redundancy between successive frames. While both MPEG-4 Part 2 (Visual) and MPEG-4 Part 10 AVC (Advanced Video Coding / H.264) rely on block-based motion estimation and compensation, AVC introduces substantial algorithmic advancements. AVC achieves significantly higher compression efficiency through variable block-size motion compensation, enhanced sub-pixel interpolation filters, multi-frame referencing, and advanced bi-directional prediction mechanisms compared to the simpler, more rigid architecture of MPEG-4 Part 2.

Block Size and Partitioning Flexibility

  • MPEG-4 Part 2: Motion compensation operates primarily on standard 16x16 pixel macroblocks. In its Advanced Simple Profile (ASP), an encoder can split a 16x16 macroblock into four 8x8 sub-blocks (inter-8x8 mode), allowing a maximum of four motion vectors per macroblock.
  • MPEG-4 AVC: Introduces Variable Block-Size Motion Compensation (VBSMC) structured hierarchically. A 16x16 macroblock can be partitioned into 16x16, 16x8, 8x16, or 8x8 blocks. Furthermore, each 8x8 block can be partitioned into 8x4, 4x8, or 4x4 sub-blocks. This allows up to 16 distinct motion vectors per macroblock, enabling precise tracking of small, non-rigid, or irregularly shaped moving objects.

Sub-Pixel Precision and Interpolation Filters

  • MPEG-4 Part 2: Standard profiles utilize half-pixel (1/2-pel) precision via simple bilinear interpolation. The Advanced Simple Profile supports quarter-pixel (1/4-pel) precision, but it computes half-pel positions using basic filtering and quarter-pel positions using simple linear averaging of adjacent integer and half-pel samples.
  • MPEG-4 AVC: Employs standard quarter-pixel (1/4-pel) precision across most profiles with a more sophisticated two-stage filtering algorithm:
    1. Half-pel sample values are calculated using a 6-tap FIR (Finite Impulse Response) filter with weights \([1, -5, 20, 20, -5, 1] / 32\), which preserves higher-frequency image details and reduces aliasing.
    2. Quarter-pel sample values are subsequently generated by bilinear interpolation between adjacent integer and half-pel samples.

Reference Frame Management

  • MPEG-4 Part 2: Uses single-frame referencing. P-frames predict exclusively from the single most recent reconstructed I- or P-frame. B-frames predict from strictly one past reference frame and one future reference frame.
  • MPEG-4 AVC: Features Multi-Reference Frame Prediction. Decoders maintain a Decoded Picture Buffer (DPB) that allows P-frames and B-frames to reference up to 16 different previously decoded frames. The reference selection is transmitted per partition, allowing an encoder to optimize prediction across scene cuts, uncovered background regions, and repetitive motions.

Generalized Bi-Prediction and Weighted Prediction

  • MPEG-4 Part 2: B-frame prediction relies on strict temporal ordering (one preceding frame and one succeeding frame) using standard arithmetic averaging: \[\text{Prediction} = \frac{\text{Forward} + \text{Backward}}{2}\]
  • MPEG-4 AVC: Introduces generalized B-slices (bi-predictive slices) and Weighted Prediction:
    • Generalized Prediction Lists: AVC maintains two reference picture lists (List 0 and List 1). A bi-predicted block can use two frames that are both temporally in the past, both in the future, or in opposite temporal directions.
    • Explicit and Implicit Weighted Prediction: Allows scaling factors and offsets to be applied to reference samples prior to motion compensation. This provides superior compression performance during fades, cross-dissolves, and illumination changes.

Motion Vector Prediction

  • MPEG-4 Part 2: Uses median prediction based on three neighboring motion vectors (left, above, and above-right) to calculate the motion vector difference (MVD).
  • MPEG-4 AVC: Retains directional median prediction but adapts it to the diverse geometry of variable partition sizes (e.g., 16x8 and 8x16 partitions use specific directional neighbors rather than the standard median). AVC also supports Direct Mode prediction (Spatial and Temporal) for B-slices to infer motion vectors without transmitting motion vector residuals.
