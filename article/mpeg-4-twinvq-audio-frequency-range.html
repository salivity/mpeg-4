MPEG-4 TwinVQ Audio Frequency Range

This article provides an overview of the operational frequency range and sampling capabilities of the MPEG-4 TwinVQ (Transform-domain Weighted Interleave Vector Quantization) audio compression scheme. It details how the codec manages audio bandwidth across various bitrates, its supported sampling rates, and its overall frequency response capabilities within the MPEG-4 standard.

Operational Frequency Capabilities

The MPEG-4 TwinVQ audio compression tool (standardized under ISO/IEC 14496-3) supports audio sampling frequencies ranging from 8 kHz up to 48 kHz. Consequently, its theoretical operational audio frequency range spans from 20 Hz up to 24 kHz (the Nyquist limit of a 48 kHz sample rate).

Depending on the operational configuration and target bitrate, the effective audio frequency range is categorized across different bandwidths:

  • Narrowband Audio: Operating at an 8 kHz sampling rate, the codec covers an effective audio frequency range from approximately 300 Hz to 3.4 kHz (or up to 4 kHz). This mode is typically optimized for low-bitrate speech transmission.
  • Wideband Audio: Operating at 16 kHz or 22.05 kHz sampling rates, the frequency range extends up to 7 kHz to 11 kHz, providing enhanced clarity for mixed speech and music content.
  • Full-Band Audio: At 44.1 kHz and 48 kHz sampling rates, the codec covers the full human audible spectrum from 20 Hz to 20 kHz (and up to 24 kHz).

Bitrate Scaling and Bandwidth Optimization

TwinVQ is designed for low-to-medium bitrate audio compression, typically operating between 6 kbps and 40 kbps per channel:

  • Ultra-Low Bitrates (6–8 kbps): The codec constrains the audio bandwidth to narrower frequency bands (under 4 kHz) to preserve intelligibility and minimize distortion.
  • Mid-Range Bitrates (16–24 kbps): The operational frequency response widens to cover frequencies up to 8–11 kHz, making it suitable for generic mono and stereo audio streaming.
  • Higher Bitrates (32–40+ kbps): The codec utilizes its transform-domain quantization to encode wide-spectrum components, capturing frequencies across the full audible range.

Through weighted interleave vector quantization and transform coding (MDCT), MPEG-4 TwinVQ dynamically allocates bits across critical frequency sub-bands, maintaining stable perceptual audio quality within its supported frequency limits.
