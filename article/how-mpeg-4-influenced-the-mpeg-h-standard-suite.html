How MPEG-4 Influenced the MPEG-H Standard Suite

The MPEG-4 standard laid the technical, architectural, and conceptual groundwork that directly enabled the development of the MPEG-H standard suite. While MPEG-4 revolutionized digital media by introducing advanced video coding (AVC/H.264), object-based audio paradigms, and flexible file containers, MPEG-H modernized these concepts for ultra-high-definition (UHD) streaming, immersive audio, and all-IP network environments. By evolving MPEG-4's core principles of modularity, compression efficiency, and interactive media, MPEG-H established the next-generation technologies: High Efficiency Video Coding (HEVC), MPEG-H 3D Audio, and MPEG Media Transport (MMT).

Evolution of Video Compression: AVC to HEVC

MPEG-4 Part 10 (Advanced Video Coding or H.264) established the modern block-based hybrid video coding architecture. It standardized core mechanisms such as variable block-size motion compensation, spatial intra-prediction, quarter-pixel motion vector precision, and advanced context-adaptive entropy coding (CABAC).

MPEG-H Part 2 (High Efficiency Video Coding or H.265) directly inherited this hybrid architecture rather than inventing a completely new paradigm. HEVC scaled the foundational concepts of AVC to handle 4K and 8K resolutions efficiently. It replaced AVC’s rigid 16x16 macroblock structure with flexible Coding Tree Units (CTUs) of up to 64x64 pixels, expanded intra-prediction modes, improved parallel processing tools, and refined the CABAC algorithms first popularized in MPEG-4.

Object-Based and Immersive Audio Architecture

MPEG-4 Part 3 (Audio) broke away from purely channel-based sound formats (such as stereo or traditional 5.1 surround) by introducing object-based audio and Structured Audio tools. It demonstrated that independent audio elements could carry distinct metadata, allowing dynamic rendering and interactivity on the playback side.

MPEG-H Part 3 (3D Audio) took this object-oriented concept and scaled it into a comprehensive system for immersive, three-dimensional sound. Building on the heritage of MPEG-4 AAC and Spatial Audio Object Coding (SAOC), MPEG-H 3D Audio supports channel-based beds, dynamic audio objects, and Higher-Order Ambisonics (HOA). This allows end-users to interact with individual audio elements—such as dialogue levels or commentary tracks—a capability directly rooted in the MPEG-4 object-based design philosophy.

Modernizing Systems and Transport

MPEG-4 introduced standardized delivery formats, notably the ISO Base Media File Format (ISOBMFF) in MPEG-4 Part 12, as well as complex scene description frameworks (BIFS). While effective for packaged media and early streaming, the transport mechanisms were heavily reliant on legacy MPEG-2 Transport Stream (MPEG-2 TS) structures for broadcast.

MPEG-H recognized the limitations of applying MPEG-2 TS to IP-centric networks. Consequently, MPEG-H Part 1 defined MPEG Media Transport (MMT). MMT adapts the container concepts of the MPEG-4 ISOBMFF to modern, packet-based IP delivery, supporting cross-layer optimizations, hybrid delivery (simultaneous broadcast and broadband), and low-latency streaming requirements.

Standard Architecture and Modularity

Beyond specific algorithms, MPEG-4 established the design pattern of breaking a massive multimedia standard into distinct, interoperable "parts" (Systems, Video, Audio, File Format). The MPEG-H suite adopted this same modular structure, ensuring that video (HEVC), audio (3D Audio), and delivery (MMT) could be developed, updated, and implemented either independently or as a unified multimedia broadcasting pipeline.
