MPEG-4 AAC Scale Factor Bands and Quantization

Scale factor bands in MPEG-4 Advanced Audio Coding (AAC) serve as the primary mechanism for perceptual noise shaping during lossy audio compression. By partitioning the frequency spectrum into groups that align with human auditory perception, scale factor bands allow the encoder to control quantization step sizes locally. This ensures that quantization distortion (noise) is dynamically distributed and kept below the psychoacoustic masking threshold across all frequencies, maximizing audio fidelity while minimizing bitrate.

The Role of Critical Bands in Psychoacoustics

Human hearing perceives frequencies non-linearly, resolving spectral details through "critical bands" that are narrower at low frequencies and wider at high frequencies. MPEG-4 AAC models this behavior by grouping Modified Discrete Cosine Transform (MDCT) spectral coefficients into scale factor bands (SFBs). Each SFB approximates a critical band of the human auditory system (typically following the Bark or Equivalent Rectangular Bandwidth scale).

Mechanism of Quantization Distortion Control

Quantization is the process where continuous MDCT coefficients are rounded to discrete values, which introduces quantization error—perceived as noise or distortion. Scale factor bands control this distortion through the following mechanisms:

  1. Independent Step-Size Control: Instead of applying a single uniform quantization step size across the entire frequency spectrum, AAC assigns an independent amplification factor (a scale factor) to each SFB. Modifying the scale factor scales the MDCT coefficients within that band prior to non-linear quantization.
  2. Noise Shaping: Adjusting a band's scale factor directly changes the quantization step size for that specific frequency region. A smaller step size reduces quantization noise in that band (at the cost of consuming more bits), while a larger step size increases quantization noise (saving bits).
  3. Auditory Masking Integration: The AAC psychoacoustic model calculates a masking threshold for each SFB—the maximum noise level that remains inaudible due to simultaneous masking by louder adjacent tones or noise components. The encoder adjusts the scale factor for each SFB so that the quantization distortion remains strictly below this computed threshold.

The Two-Loop Bit Allocation Process

MPEG-4 AAC uses a dual-loop optimization algorithm to balance bitrate constraints and distortion control using scale factor bands:

  • Inner Loop (Rate Control): Adjusts a global gain parameter across all bands to ensure the total number of bits required to encode the quantized coefficients matches the target bit budget.
  • Outer Loop (Distortion Control): Measures the resulting quantization error in each scale factor band against the psychoacoustic masking threshold. If the distortion in a specific band exceeds the audible threshold, the outer loop decreases the scale factor for that band to reduce the noise, iteratively redistributing the available bits until distortion is imperceptible or minimized uniformly.

Through this localized control, scale factor bands prevent high-frequency or high-energy components from dictating the noise floor of quieter, sensitive frequency regions, ensuring optimal perceptual audio quality.
