MPEG-4 Royalty Fees for Consumer Playback Devices

Manufacturers of consumer hardware with MPEG-4 decoding capabilities must license essential patents managed by licensing administrators like MPEG LA (now operating within Via Licensing Alliance). Royalty fees for consumer hardware playback devices are structured on a tiered, per-unit basis according to annual shipment volumes. These structures include initial royalty-free exemptions and annual enterprise caps to limit total financial liability, with rates varying depending on whether the device implements MPEG-4 Visual (Part 2) or MPEG-4 AVC/H.264 (Part 10).

MPEG-4 Visual (Part 2) Playback Royalties

For standalone hardware products incorporating MPEG-4 Visual decoding or playback capabilities:

  • 0 to 50,000 units per year: $0.00 (royalty-free tier).
  • Above 50,000 units per year: $0.25 per unit.
  • Annual Maximum (Enterprise Cap): Royalties are capped at approximately $1.2 million per year per legal entity, ensuring large-scale manufacturers do not pay past this ceiling in a single calendar year.

MPEG-4 AVC / H.264 (Part 10) Playback Royalties

Devices capable of decoding MPEG-4 AVC (the widely used H.264 standard) are subject to a separate, tiered royalty schedule:

  • 0 to 100,000 units per year: $0.00 (royalty-free tier).
  • 100,001 to 5,000,000 units per year: $0.20 per unit.
  • Above 5,000,000 units per year: $0.10 per unit.
  • Annual Maximum (Enterprise Cap): The portfolio license enforces an annual enterprise cap (historically scaling between $6.5 million and $10.5 million across different calendar terms) covering all hardware units sold by a company and its operating subsidiaries.

Licensing Terms and Scope

  • Applicable Devices: The licensing terms apply to any consumer hardware device containing an MPEG-4 hardware or firmware decoder, including smart televisions, set-top boxes, Blu-ray players, digital cameras, smartphones, and media streaming players.
  • Payee Responsibility: The royalty is typically payable by the brand owner or original equipment manufacturer (OEM) selling the end-user device.
  • Hardware Integration: If an OEM purchases a pre-licensed decoder chip or subsystem from a fully licensed semiconductor supplier, the royalty obligation for that specific functionality may already be satisfied, avoiding double taxation under the standard licensing agreement.
