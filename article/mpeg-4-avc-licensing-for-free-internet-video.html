MPEG-4 AVC Licensing for Free Internet Video

The MPEG LA licensing structure grants a permanent royalty exemption for MPEG-4 AVC (H.264) video content that is broadcast or streamed over the internet for free to the end consumer. While content creators and platforms do not pay royalties for distributing free video, the exemption applies strictly to the content stream itself rather than the hardware, software, and tools used to encode and decode the video.

The Royalty-Free Provision for Free Internet Video

In 2010, MPEG LA announced that it would not charge royalties for AVC-encoded video that is transmitted over the internet without charging end users. This policy applies to:

  • Free, ad-supported streaming services (AVOD)
  • Publicly accessible platforms (such as YouTube, Twitch, and Vimeo)
  • Free live webcasts and video conferences
  • Promotional, corporate, and personal video hosted online

Initially announced with an expiration date, MPEG LA later made this exemption permanent for the entire duration of the AVC Patent Portfolio License. Consequently, content distributors do not need to track viewer numbers or pay transmission fees for free video broadcasts.

Paid Content vs. Free Content

The royalty exemption applies only when the viewer is not charged to watch the video. If the business model involves direct viewer payments, royalties apply under MPEG LA's Title-by-Title or Subscription models:

  • Pay-Per-View and Transactional (TVOD): Transmissions that charge users per view or download are subject to royalties per title or viewing event, subject to annual maximum limits.
  • Subscription Services (SVOD): Platforms charging recurring subscription fees for access to AVC video content must pay royalties based on subscriber tiers.

Distinction Between Content and Codec Implementation

A critical distinction in the MPEG LA framework is the difference between distributing content and distributing software or hardware:

  • Content Distribution: Free internet video streams carry no licensing fees.
  • Encoders and Decoders: Companies manufacturing, selling, or distributing software or hardware capable of encoding or decoding MPEG-4 AVC video remain subject to MPEG LA licensing terms and unit royalties.

In practice, individual video creators and free streaming platforms rely on licensed encoder and decoder implementations provided by operating systems, browsers, camera manufacturers, or software vendors, who handle the underlying patent royalties.
