MPEG-4 Audio Sampling Rates: 8 kHz to 96 kHz

The MPEG-4 General Audio (GA) toolset provides a flexible framework designed to handle audio sampling rates ranging from 8 kHz up to 96 kHz. By integrating scalable framing structures, adaptive psychoacoustic modeling, explicit frequency signaling, and modular tools like Spectral Band Replication (SBR), the MPEG-4 standard dynamically adapts its coding efficiency and frequency resolution to accommodate low-bitrate voice, broadcast-quality audio, and ultra-high-definition studio streams.

Sampling Frequency Signaling and Configuration

The MPEG-4 GA architecture—predominantly centered around Advanced Audio Coding (AAC)—communicates the operating sampling frequency within its initial configuration header (AudioSpecificConfig). The decoder reads a 4-bit samplingFrequencyIndex field representing standard predefined sample rates:

  • High-Definition Rates (64 kHz, 88.2 kHz, 96 kHz): Used for studio-grade reproduction and preserving ultrasonic frequencies.
  • Standard Audio Rates (32 kHz, 44.1 kHz, 48 kHz): Standard consumer distribution rates for music and multimedia.
  • Low-Bitrate/Speech Rates (8 kHz, 11.025 kHz, 12 kHz, 16 kHz, 22.05 kHz, 24 kHz): Optimized for low-bandwidth communication and narrow/wideband speech.

If an arbitrary, non-standard rate is required within the 8–96 kHz bounds, an escape value allows the encoder to explicitly transmit a 24-bit direct sampling frequency value.

Adaptive Time-to-Frequency Transform and Framing

MPEG-4 GA uses the Modified Discrete Cosine Transform (MDCT) as its primary filter bank. In standard AAC configurations, frame lengths are fixed in sample count (typically 1024 or 960 samples for long blocks, and 128 or 120 samples for short blocks).

Because the sample count per frame remains constant:

  • At 96 kHz: A 1024-sample frame represents a brief time window (~10.67 ms), delivering high temporal resolution naturally suited for transient-heavy, high-definition audio.
  • At 8 kHz: A 1024-sample frame represents a much longer duration (~128 ms), maximizing frequency resolution and coding efficiency where channel bandwidth is constrained.

Dynamic Scale Factor Band Allocation

Human auditory perception varies based on the audible bandwidth available at a given sampling rate. The MPEG-4 toolset employs sample-rate-specific lookup tables to determine the partitioning of MDCT spectral lines into Scale Factor Bands (SFBs).

These tables mimic the human ear’s critical bands (Bark scale):

  • At 8 kHz, the spectrum is divided into fewer, narrower SFBs across a 4 kHz Nyquist bandwidth.
  • At 96 kHz, the spectrum is divided into a greater number of SFBs spanning up to a 48 kHz Nyquist limit, allowing precise quantization noise shaping across both the audible and ultrasonic spectra.

Modular Tools and Hierarchical Bandwidth Management

To maintain high perceptual quality across the entire 8 kHz to 96 kHz spectrum, MPEG-4 GA incorporates hierarchical and bandwidth-extension tools:

  • Spectral Band Replication (SBR): In profiles such as HE-AAC, the core coder operates at a lower sampling rate (e.g., 24 kHz or 48 kHz), while the SBR tool reconstructs the upper spectral envelope at the decoder to output 48 kHz or 96 kHz audio, conserving bitrate without sacrificing high-frequency reproduction.
  • Sampling Rate Switching and Scalability: MPEG-4 enables bitstream scalability where a base layer can decode at a lower sampling frequency (e.g., 16 kHz for basic intelligibility), while enhancement layers add spectral lines to reconstruct higher sample rates (e.g., 48 kHz or 96 kHz) on capable decoders.
