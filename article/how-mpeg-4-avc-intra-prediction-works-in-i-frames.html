How MPEG-4 AVC Intra-Prediction Works in I-Frames

MPEG-4 AVC (also known as H.264) reduces spatial redundancies in I-frames through spatial domain intra-prediction, a technique that predicts the sample values of a current block using the previously decoded neighboring boundary pixels from the top and left. Unlike older standards that perform prediction only in the frequency domain, AVC models spatial correlations in the pixel domain across various directional modes, computes the difference between the original and predicted pixels, and transmits only the resulting residual data.

The Mechanism of Spatial Intra-Prediction

In intra-coded frames (I-frames), temporal prediction across different frames is not used. To compress image data, MPEG-4 AVC exploits the high correlation between adjacent pixels within the same frame.

Before a block is encoded, the encoder gathers reconstructed pixel samples from the adjacent blocks directly to the left, top-left, top, and top-right. These boundary pixels serve as reference points to estimate the pixel values of the current target block. The encoder evaluates multiple prediction modes, selects the one that produces the smallest prediction error (residual), and encodes both the mode choice and the residual.

Luma Prediction Modes

MPEG-4 AVC dynamically chooses block sizes based on image complexity to balance precision and signaling overhead for luma (brightness) components:

1. Intra 4x4 Prediction

Designed for high-detail and textured regions, the 16x16 macroblock is divided into sixteen 4x4 sub-blocks. Each 4x4 block can utilize one of nine prediction modes:

  • Mode 0 (Vertical): Extrapolates samples vertically from the upper boundary pixels.
  • Mode 1 (Horizontal): Extrapolates samples horizontally from the left boundary pixels.
  • Mode 2 (DC): Averages the available upper and left boundary pixels to fill the block with a single mean value.
  • Modes 3 to 8 (Directional/Diagonal): Extrapolate pixels along specific diagonal angles (e.g., Diagonal Down-Left, Diagonal Down-Right, Vertical-Right, Horizontal-Down, Vertical-Left, and Horizontal-Up) to accurately follow diagonal edges.

2. Intra 16x16 Prediction

Designed for smooth, flat, or uniform areas, this mode predicts the entire 16x16 macroblock at once, reducing the overhead of transmitting multiple mode indicators. It offers four prediction modes:

  • Vertical: Copies top boundary pixels downward.
  • Horizontal: Copies left boundary pixels across.
  • DC: Fills the macroblock with the average of top and left pixels.
  • Plane: Applies a linear interpolation formula across the horizontal and vertical directions, creating a smooth gradient.

3. Intra 8x8 Prediction

Introduced in the High Profile of AVC, Intra 8x8 provides a compromise between 4x4 and 16x16 modes. It utilizes the same nine directional modes as the 4x4 prediction but applies them to 8x8 blocks, utilizing an 8x8 integer transform to improve compression efficiency in detailed areas.

Chroma Prediction

For color representation (typically in 4:2:0 format), the chroma components (\(U\) and \(V\)) of a macroblock consist of two 8x8 blocks. Both chroma blocks share the same prediction mode, which is selected independently of the luma mode. Chroma prediction supports four modes identical in concept to the Intra 16x16 luma modes: DC, Horizontal, Vertical, and Plane.

Generating and Encoding the Residual

Once the best prediction mode is selected:

  1. Subtraction: The predicted pixel block is subtracted from the original pixel block to produce a residual matrix.
  2. Transform and Quantization: The residual matrix undergoes an integer DCT-like transform to concentrate the energy, followed by quantization to discard perceptually insignificant details.
  3. Entropy Coding: The quantized coefficients and the chosen intra-prediction mode indices are encoded into the final bitstream using CAVLC (Context-Adaptive Variable-Length Coding) or CABAC (Context-Adaptive Binary Arithmetic Coding).

By utilizing these multi-directional spatial modes, MPEG-4 AVC preserves sharp edges and smooth gradients within I-frames, achieving significantly higher compression efficiency than legacy intra-coding techniques.
