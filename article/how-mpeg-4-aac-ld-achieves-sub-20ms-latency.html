How MPEG-4 AAC-LD Achieves Sub-20ms Latency

MPEG-4 AAC-LD (Low Delay) is an audio coding standard designed to deliver full-bandwidth, high-fidelity audio for real-time, two-way communication systems like video conferencing and VoIP. Standard perceptual audio codecs typically introduce latencies exceeding 100 milliseconds, which disrupts natural conversation. AAC-LD successfully reduces algorithmic encoding latency to under 20 milliseconds through four primary architectural modifications: reducing frame size, eliminating block switching, optimizing filter bank overlap, and constraining the bit reservoir.

1. Reduced Frame Size

Standard MPEG-4 AAC uses a default transform block size of 1024 samples (or 960 samples). At a standard 48 kHz sampling rate, capturing a 1024-sample frame requires approximately 21.3 milliseconds of buffering time before the encoder can even begin processing the data.

AAC-LD cuts the transform frame size in half, supporting either 480 or 512 samples:

  • A 512-sample frame requires 10.67 ms of buffering at 48 kHz.
  • A 480-sample frame requires 10.0 ms of buffering at 48 kHz (or 15 ms at 32 kHz).

Halving the frame duration cuts the fundamental buffering latency in half while retaining sufficient frequency resolution for perceptual audio coding.

2. Low-Overlap Filter Bank (Eliminating Look-Ahead)

Standard AAC uses a Modified Discrete Cosine Transform (MDCT) with a 50% overlap-add structure. To process a 1024-sample frame, standard AAC requires a 2048-sample window, creating an inherent look-ahead delay equal to the frame length.

AAC-LD replaces standard windowing with an asymmetric low-overlap MDCT filter bank. By reshaping the transform window, the codec eliminates or substantially minimizes the look-ahead window into future audio samples. The algorithmic look-ahead delay is reduced to a fraction of a frame, allowing the encoder to transform and quantize audio nearly instantaneously once the current frame's samples are buffered.

3. Removal of Window Switching

Standard AAC switches dynamically between long windows (for steady-state signals) and sequences of eight short windows (to prevent pre-echo artifacts on transient sounds). Determining when to switch windows requires analyzing upcoming audio data, adding additional look-ahead delay.

AAC-LD eliminates window switching entirely. Instead, it relies on a fixed window length and uses Temporal Noise Shaping (TNS) along with optimized frequency domain quantization to manage transient audio without requiring future-sample look-ahead buffers.

4. Elimination of the Bit Reservoir

Standard perceptual codecs use a bit reservoir to distribute bits across variable-complexity audio segments. Complex passages borrow bits from simpler passages, requiring receiver-side smoothing buffers that introduce decoding latency.

AAC-LD strictly minimizes or eliminates the bit reservoir. Each frame is encoded within a tightly bound bit budget, ensuring that frames can be transmitted, received, and decoded immediately without waiting for buffer accumulation.

Total Algorithmic Latency

Algorithmic delay consists solely of the frame buffer time plus the filter bank look-ahead time. With a 480-sample frame at 48 kHz (10 ms) combined with minimal filter bank look-ahead overhead, MPEG-4 AAC-LD achieves an algorithmic latency between 10 ms and 15 ms, well below the 20 ms threshold required for imperceptible latency in interactive communications.
