MPEG-4 Motion Vector Limits Per Macroblock

This article details the exact limits on motion vectors (MVs) per macroblock across MPEG-4 profiles and levels. In MPEG-4 standards—including MPEG-4 Part 2 (Visual) and MPEG-4 Part 10 (AVC/H.264)—motion vector constraints are defined in two ways: the structural count of motion vectors allowed per macroblock (determined primarily by profile partition capabilities) and the vertical/horizontal displacement ranges (strictly bounded by level specifications).

MPEG-4 Part 2 (Visual)

MPEG-4 Part 2 macroblocks are \(16 \times 16\) pixels. The maximum number of motion vectors per macroblock depends on whether the profile allows 4MV (Advanced Prediction) and B-VOP (bidirectional) modes.

1. Motion Vector Count by Profile

  • Simple Profile (SP) (Levels 0–6):
    • P-VOPs: Maximum 1 MV per macroblock (\(16 \times 16\)).
    • B-VOPs: Not supported.
  • Advanced Simple Profile (ASP) (Levels 0–5):
    • P-VOPs (1MV mode): 1 MV per macroblock (\(16 \times 16\)).
    • P-VOPs (4MV mode): Up to 4 MVs per macroblock (one for each \(8 \times 8\) sub-block).
    • B-VOPs: Up to 4 forward and 4 backward MVs (8 total MVs per macroblock) in \(8 \times 8\) bidirectional mode.

2. Motion Vector Displacement Limits Across Levels (Part 2)

Motion vector search range in MPEG-4 Part 2 is governed by the level-dependent f_code value (motion range parameter).

Level Max Horizontal / Vertical Range (Half-Pel Units) Max Displacement (Pixels)
Level 0 f_code = 1 \(\pm 32\)
Level 1 f_code = 1 \(\pm 32\)
Level 2 f_code = 2 \(\pm 64\)
Level 3 f_code = 3 \(\pm 128\)
Level 4 f_code = 4 \(\pm 256\)
Level 5 f_code = 5 \(\pm 512\)

MPEG-4 Part 10 / AVC (H.264)

In MPEG-4 AVC, macroblocks can be partitioned down to \(4 \times 4\) sub-blocks.

1. Motion Vector Count per Macroblock

The standard allows up to 16 partitions (\(4 \times 4\)) per \(16 \times 16\) macroblock:

  • P-Slices (Inter): Up to 16 MVs per macroblock (1 per \(4 \times 4\) sub-partition, list 0).
  • B-Slices (Bi-predictive): Up to 32 MVs per macroblock (1 forward from list 0 and 1 backward from list 1 per \(4 \times 4\) sub-partition).

Note: There is no level that explicitly caps the partition count below 16/32; all compliant AVC decoders supporting inter-prediction must handle up to 32 motion vectors per macroblock.

2. Maximum Vertical Vector Component Limits Across Levels (MaxVmvR)

While horizontal vector limits are constrained by frame boundaries, MPEG-4 Part 10 explicitly restricts the maximum vertical motion vector displacement (MaxVmvR) per level in units of quarter-luma samples.
