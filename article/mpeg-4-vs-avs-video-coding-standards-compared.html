MPEG-4 vs AVS: Video Coding Standards Compared

This article provides a comparative analysis of the MPEG-4 standard and China’s Audio Video Coding Standard (AVS). It evaluates the two video compression technologies across their architectural design, coding efficiency, licensing structures, and real-world market adoption, highlighting why China developed its own standard as an alternative to international formats.

Origins and Strategic Objectives

MPEG-4 was established by the Moving Picture Experts Group (ISO/IEC) in the late 1990s and early 2000s, encompassing various profiles, most notably MPEG-4 Part 2 and MPEG-4 Part 10 (Advanced Video Coding / H.264). Its goal was to provide high-efficiency compression suitable for everything from low-bitrate mobile streaming to high-definition broadcasting.

AVS was initiated in 2002 by the Chinese government through the AVS Working Group. The primary motivation was strategic and economic: to reduce China's heavy reliance on foreign patents and eliminate the steep licensing fees associated with international standards like MPEG-4 AVC/H.264.

Technical Performance and Compression Efficiency

When comparing MPEG-4 Part 10 (H.264/AVC) to the initial standard AVS1-P2 (Jizhun profile):

  • Compression Ratio: AVS1 offers compression efficiency roughly comparable to the Main Profile of MPEG-4 AVC/H.264, achieving standard and high-definition video at bitrates roughly half of MPEG-2.
  • Algorithmic Complexity: AVS was engineered to have lower computational complexity than MPEG-4 AVC. It uses simpler 8x8 integer transforms and 2D variable-length coding, compared to H.264's more complex Context-Adaptive Binary Arithmetic Coding (CABAC) and mixed transform sizes. This makes AVS encoding and decoding slightly less resource-intensive.
  • Evolution: Just as MPEG evolved to H.265/HEVC and H.266/VVC, the AVS family expanded with AVS2 and AVS3. AVS2 matches HEVC efficiency for 4K video, while AVS3 targets 8K and competitive performance with VVC.

Licensing and Royalty Models

The most significant differentiator between MPEG-4 and AVS lies in intellectual property management:

  • MPEG-4: Administered primarily via MPEG LA (now Via Licensing Alliance), the licensing model for MPEG-4 Part 10 involves patent pools with multiple tiers of royalties, device fees, and potentially content distribution fees. This structure led to high cumulative costs for Chinese hardware manufacturers.
  • AVS: AVS uses a localized, transparent, and significantly cheaper licensing scheme. Royalties are typically fixed at a nominal one-time fee per device (roughly 1 RMB, or approximately $0.15 USD), with zero royalty fees charged on content or broadcasting.

Market Adoption and Ecosystem

MPEG-4, particularly Part 10 (H.264), is the dominant global standard. It is natively supported by virtually every modern consumer device, web browser, operating system, and streaming platform worldwide.

AVS, while standardized internationally by the IEEE and recognized by the ITU-T, is predominantly deployed within mainland China. It serves as the mandatory or primary standard for Chinese digital terrestrial television, satellite broadcasting, cable television networks, and domestic surveillance systems. Outside of domestic Chinese broadcast and specific regional export markets, global hardware and software ecosystems continue to rely overwhelmingly on MPEG-based formats.
