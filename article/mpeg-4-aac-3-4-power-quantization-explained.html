MPEG-4 AAC 3/4-Power Quantization Explained

MPEG-4 Advanced Audio Coding (AAC) achieves high-fidelity audio compression by transforming time-domain signals into frequency-domain spectral coefficients via the Modified Discrete Cosine Transform (MDCT) and quantizing them efficiently. A central component of this process is non-uniform quantization utilizing a 3/4-power companding rule. By compressing larger spectral amplitudes and preserving higher precision for lower amplitudes, this mechanism aligns quantization noise with the human psychoacoustic masking threshold, minimizing perceptual distortion while maximizing data compression.

The Principle of 3/4-Power Companding

Quantization in digital audio traditionally introduces quantization noise across the spectrum. A linear (uniform) quantizer applies the same step size across all amplitudes, which is inefficient for audio signals because the human auditory system is significantly more sensitive to errors in quiet sounds than in loud sounds.

AAC solves this using companding—a combination of compressing the dynamic range prior to quantization and expanding it during decoding. The standard specifies a power-law function with an exponent of \(3/4\) (\(0.75\)).

Applying a power of \(3/4\) yields a non-linear transfer curve:

  • Small spectral values receive a steeper slope, resulting in smaller effective step sizes and higher precision.
  • Large spectral values are compressed along a flatter slope, resulting in larger step sizes and coarser resolution.

This ensures that the signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) roughly tracks the signal amplitude, keeping the noise floor below the psychoacoustic masking threshold.

The Forward Quantization Formula

In the MPEG-4 AAC encoder, MDCT spectral coefficients \(X[k]\) are partitioned into Scale Factor Bands (SFBs). For a given coefficient \(X[k]\) within a band controlled by a scale factor \(sf\), the forward quantization equation is defined as:

\[X_{quant}[k] = \text{sgn}(X[k]) \cdot \left\lfloor \left( |X[k]| \cdot 2^{-\frac{sf}{4}} \right)^{\frac{3}{4}} + 0.4054 \right\rfloor\]

Key variables in this process include:

  • \(|X[k]|\): The magnitude of the MDCT frequency coefficient.
  • \(2^{-\frac{sf}{4}}\): The scale factor gain adjustment. Each integer increment of \(sf\) alters the gain by \(1.505\text{ dB}\) (\(2^{1/4}\) or \(\sqrt[4]{2}\) ratio).
  • \((\dots)^{3/4}\): The non-linear companding power operation.
  • \(+ 0.4054\): A rounding offset (derived from \(\approx 0.5 - \text{bias}\)) that optimizes the mean square error and mitigates bias in integer truncation.
  • \(\lfloor \dots \rfloor\): The floor function converting the value into an integer for subsequent Huffman entropy coding.

Role of Scale Factor Bands

The 3/4-power compander operates in tandem with AAC's scale factor mechanism. Spectral coefficients are grouped into bands that mimic the critical bands of human hearing (Bark or ERB scales).

  1. Psychoacoustic Analysis: The encoder calculates a Signal-to-Mask Ratio (SMR) for each scale factor band to determine how much noise can be safely introduced without becoming audible.
  2. Gain Adaptation: The scale factor \(sf\) scales the input coefficients up or down before the 3/4 exponent is applied.
  3. Loop Optimization: Rate-distortion loops (often implemented via a Two-Loop Search algorithm) iteratively adjust \(sf\) across bands until the quantized integers meet both the target bit budget and the perceptual noise limits.

Inverse Quantization at the Decoder

At the decoder stage, the integer values \(X_{quant}[k]\) are decoded from the bitstream using Huffman tables and then reconstructed to their approximate continuous spectral amplitudes.

The decoder reverses the companding operation using the inverse exponent, \(4/3\) (\(1.333\dots\)):

\[\hat{X}[k] = \text{sgn}(X_{quant}[k]) \cdot |X_{quant}[k]|^{\frac{4}{3}} \cdot 2^{\frac{sf}{4}}\]

Because the \(4/3\)-power expansion steepens rapidly for larger integer values, the larger intervals are restored to their original wide dynamic range, completing the non-uniform reconstruction while keeping the bitstream representation compact.
