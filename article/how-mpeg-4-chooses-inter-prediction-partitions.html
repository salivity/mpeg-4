How MPEG-4 Chooses Inter-Prediction Partitions

This article explains how an MPEG-4 encoder (specifically MPEG-4 Part 10 / H.264 AVC) decides between 16x16, 8x8, and 4x4 partition sizes during inter-frame prediction. The selection is driven by a trade-off between motion accuracy and bitrate overhead, systematically resolved using Motion Estimation and Rate-Distortion Optimization (RDO).

The Purpose of Variable Block Size Partitions

Inter-prediction reduces temporal redundancy between successive video frames by tracking movement via motion vectors (MVs). Different regions of a frame require different levels of detail:

  • 16x16 Partitions: Ideal for large, uniform areas with consistent motion, such as flat backgrounds or panning scenes. They require fewer bits to signal motion vectors.
  • 8x8 and 4x4 Partitions: Necessary for complex motion, fine textures, and moving object edges. Smaller partitions reduce prediction error (residual energy) at the cost of requiring more bits to transmit multiple motion vectors.

Step-by-Step Decision Process

1. Hierarchical Motion Estimation

The encoder performs motion estimation across various block configurations in a hierarchical tree structure:

  • Macroblock Level: The encoder tests partitions such as 16x16, 16x8, 8x16, and 8x8.
  • Sub-Macroblock Level: If an 8x8 partition is considered, each 8x8 block can be further subdivided into 8x8, 8x4, 4x8, or 4x4 sub-partitions.

For each tested partition, the encoder searches reference frames to find the best motion vector that minimizes prediction error.

2. Cost Function Calculation

To determine which partition size is most efficient, the encoder calculates a cost metric for each configuration. Fast encoders often use the Sum of Absolute Differences (SAD) or Sum of Absolute Transformed Differences (SATD), while high-efficiency encoders use full Rate-Distortion Optimization (RDO).

The standard RDO cost formula is:

\[J = D + \lambda \cdot R\]

  • \(J\) (Cost): The Lagrangian cost to be minimized.
  • \(D\) (Distortion): The difference between the original block and the reconstructed block, typically measured using Sum of Squared Errors (SSE).
  • \(R\) (Rate): The exact number of bits required to encode the mode decision, motion vectors, and residual transform coefficients.
  • \(\lambda\) (Lagrange Multiplier): A weighting factor derived from the quantization parameter (QP) that balances image fidelity against bit budget.

3. Bottom-Up and Top-Down Comparison

In full RDO mode, the encoder computes the cost for the smallest sub-blocks first:

  1. Sub-Macroblock Evaluation: For each 8x8 block, the encoder calculates the cost \(J\) for combinations of 8x8, 8x4, 4x8, and 4x4. The configuration with the lowest cost is chosen to represent that 8x8 block.
  2. Macroblock Evaluation: The encoder sums the costs of the four best 8x8 sub-partitions and compares that total against the costs of the larger partition modes (16x16, 16x8, and 8x16), as well as Intra-prediction modes.

4. Final Selection

The encoder selects the partitioning structure that yields the minimum total cost \(J\). If the bit savings of a single 16x16 motion vector outweigh the distortion penalty, the 16x16 partition is chosen. Conversely, if complex motion causes a large residual error that requires fine-grained prediction, the encoder commits to 8x8 or 4x4 partitions.
