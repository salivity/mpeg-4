Why IP Cameras Use MPEG 4 AVC Sub-Streams

Modern IP surveillance cameras frequently output dual or multiple video feeds simultaneously, pairing a high-resolution primary stream with a lower-resolution MPEG-4 AVC (H.264) sub-stream. This article examines the technical and operational reasons for this design, detailing how multi-streaming manages network bandwidth, reduces client-side processing loads, guarantees broad device compatibility, and optimizes video management software performance.

Reducing Network Bandwidth and Latency

High-resolution primary streams—such as 4K, 5MP, or 1080p feeds—generate massive amounts of data. Transmitting these full-resolution streams continuously over local networks or the internet can quickly saturate bandwidth, especially in environments with multiple cameras or constrained remote connections.

A sub-stream operates at a significantly reduced resolution (such as D1, VGA, or 720p) and lower frame rate. By utilizing a lower bitrate MPEG-4 AVC sub-stream for live monitoring and mobile viewing, systems prevent network congestion while preserving the full-resolution primary stream for local storage and forensic analysis.

Lowering Client-Side Hardware Decoding Demands

Displaying multiple high-resolution video streams on a single monitoring station requires immense processing power. If a security operator views a grid of 16 or 32 cameras simultaneously in 4K, the client machine's CPU and GPU would quickly become overwhelmed, leading to dropped frames, latency, or software crashes.

Video Management Systems (VMS) and Network Video Recorders (NVRs) solve this by automatically pulling the lightweight MPEG-4 AVC sub-stream when cameras are displayed in multi-view grid layouts. Because the display tile for each camera in a grid is physically small on the screen, the lower resolution of the sub-stream is visually imperceptible. The system only switches to the primary stream when an operator selects a single camera for full-screen viewing.

Universal Compatibility and Legacy Support

While modern primary streams often use newer codecs like H.265 (HEVC) or AV1 to maximize storage efficiency, these formats require specialized hardware decoding that may not be supported by older client devices, web browsers, or legacy hardware decoders.

MPEG-4 AVC (H.264) remains the global standard for digital video with near-universal hardware acceleration across virtually all smartphones, web platforms, and operating systems. Providing an MPEG-4 AVC sub-stream ensures that users can access live video feeds reliably through web browsers, mobile applications, and third-party integrations without requiring specialized proprietary plugins or advanced decoding hardware.

Efficient Motion Detection and Edge Analytics

Many IP camera systems utilize the sub-stream to process server-side or client-side video analytics and motion detection. Analyzing a lower-resolution MPEG-4 AVC feed drastically reduces the mathematical complexity and processing overhead required to detect motion, read patterns, or trigger alarms, allowing analytics engines to run faster and scale across more channels simultaneously.
