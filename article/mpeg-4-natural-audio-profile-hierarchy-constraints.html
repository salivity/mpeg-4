MPEG-4 Natural Audio Profile Hierarchy Constraints

The MPEG-4 standard (ISO/IEC 14496-3) organizes its audio compression capabilities into specific profiles and levels to ensure interoperability across various hardware and network limitations. Natural Audio profiles define subsets of standardized Audio Object Types (AOTs) optimized for voice, high-fidelity music, low-latency communication, and scalable delivery. This guide details the structural hierarchy of these profiles alongside the strict operational constraints imposed on sampling rates, channel counts, bitrates, and computational complexity.

The MPEG-4 Profile and Level Framework

MPEG-4 Audio differentiates between two structural definitions:

  • Profiles: Specify the exact combination of coding tools and Audio Object Types (AOTs) a decoder must support.
  • Levels: Specify the quantitative bounds—such as maximum sample rates, maximum number of channels, and decoder buffer sizes—within a given profile.

Core Profiles in the Natural Audio Hierarchy

The Natural Audio hierarchy scales from narrow-band speech coding up to full-bandwidth, multi-channel general audio.

1. Speech Audio Profile

Designed strictly for ultra-low bitrate voice communications.

  • Allowed Audio Object Types: Harmonic Vector Excitation Coding (HVXC), Code-Excited Linear Prediction (CELP), and Silence Insertion Description (SID).
  • Target Bitrates: 2 kbit/s to 4 kbit/s (HVXC) and 4 kbit/s to 24 kbit/s (CELP).
  • Sampling Rate Constraints: Restricted to 8 kHz (narrowband) and 16 kHz (wideband).
  • Channel Limit: Typically restricted to 1 channel (mono).

2. Low Delay Audio Profile

Optimized for bidirectional, real-time communications requiring an algorithmic delay under 20 ms.

  • Allowed Audio Object Types: AAC Low Delay (AAC-LD), Error Resilient (ER) CELP, and ER HVXC.
  • Sampling Rate Constraints: 8 kHz to 48 kHz.
  • Channel Constraints: Mono up to Stereo (2.0) depending on the defined level.

3. Advanced Audio Coding (AAC) Profile

Dedicated to general broadcast, storage, and streaming without speech-specific algorithmic tools.

  • Allowed Audio Object Types: AAC Low Complexity (AAC LC), AAC Long Term Prediction (AAC LTP), and AAC Scalable.
  • Sampling Rate Constraints: 8 kHz up to 96 kHz.
  • Channel Constraints: Up to 48 discrete audio channels (most common levels target 2.0 stereo or 5.1 surround).

4. High Quality Audio Profile

Builds on high-fidelity tools with added error-resilience features for transmission over unreliable networks.

  • Allowed Audio Object Types: AAC LC, AAC LTP, AAC Scalable, ER AAC LC, ER AAC LTP, ER AAC Scalable, and TwinVQ.
  • Sampling Rate Constraints: 8 kHz up to 96 kHz.
  • Channel Constraints: Mono, stereo, and multi-channel configurations up to level maximums.

5. Scalable Audio Profile

Provides layered decoding where a base layer provides standard quality and enhancement layers add fidelity or bandwidth.

  • Allowed Audio Object Types: CELP (base speech layer), AAC Scalable, TwinVQ, and BSAC (Bit-Sliced Arithmetic Coding).
  • Scalability Range: Continuous bitrates from 6 kbit/s (speech base) to beyond 64 kbit/s per channel (full audio bandwidth).
  • Sampling Rate Constraints: 8 kHz to 48 kHz.

6. Main Audio Profile

The superset of natural coding tools within the MPEG-4 standard.

  • Allowed Audio Object Types: Includes all AOTs from the Speech, Scalable, AAC, and High Quality profiles (AAC Main, AAC LC, AAC LTP, AAC Scalable, TwinVQ, CELP, HVXC, and Text-to-Speech Interface / TTSI).
  • Constraint Rule: A compliant Main Audio Profile decoder must be backward-compatible with bitstreams encoded under the Speech, Scalable, and High Quality profiles.

Quantitative Level Constraints

Each profile is bound by numeric Levels (typically Level 1 through Level 4), which impose the following limits:

Level Max Sample Rate Max Channels Typical Max Bitrate per Channel
Level 1 24 kHz – 48 kHz 1 – 2 (Mono/Stereo) 64 kbit/s
Level 2 48 kHz 2 (Stereo) 128 kbit/s
Level 3 48 kHz 2 – 5.1 Channels 192 kbit/s
Level 4 Up to 96 kHz 5.1 – 7.1 Channels Profile Dependent (> 320 kbit/s)

Decoders must implement the specified profile's exact toolset without exceeding the memory, processing complexity, and channel limits defined by their target level tier.
