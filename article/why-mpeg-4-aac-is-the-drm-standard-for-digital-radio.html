Why MPEG-4 AAC Is the DRM Standard for Digital Radio

This article examines why the Digital Radio Mondiale (DRM) standard mandates MPEG-4 AAC as its core audio coding system. Digital radio broadcasters rely on MPEG-4 Advanced Audio Coding (AAC)—specifically its high-efficiency profiles, HE-AAC and xHE-AAC—to deliver clear audio over limited frequency bandwidths. Key factors behind this selection include superior compression efficiency at ultra-low bitrates, advanced error resilience against broadcast interference, dynamic adaptability for mixed content, and widespread hardware support across global consumer electronics.

Superior Compression Efficiency at Low Bitrates

Digital Radio Mondiale operates within the congested AM (shortwave, mediumwave, longwave) and VHF/FM radio bands, where transmission channels are typically limited to bandwidths of 9 kHz, 10 kHz, or 20 kHz. These narrow channels restrict data rates to between 6 kbps and 30 kbps.

MPEG-4 AAC profiles, notably HE-AAC (High-Efficiency AAC) and Extended HE-AAC (xHE-AAC), utilize advanced technologies such as Spectral Band Replication (SBR) and Parametric Stereo (PS). These tools reconstruct high-frequency details and stereo imaging using minimal data, allowing DRM to broadcast full stereo music and crisp speech within bandwidth limits that traditional audio codecs cannot support.

Robust Error Resilience for Wireless Transmission

Radio frequency signals are subject to multipath fading, atmospheric noise, and physical interference. MPEG-4 AAC incorporates standardized error protection and error concealment mechanisms specifically designed for volatile transmission channels. When signal packets are dropped or corrupted, the decoder conceals the missing audio rather than producing harsh digital artifacts or dropouts. This ensures that the listening experience remains smooth, even in fringe reception areas.

Dynamic Handling of Speech and Music via xHE-AAC

Broadcasting involves a constant shift between speech (news, interviews) and music. The latest evolution of the standard used in DRM, xHE-AAC, unifies specialized speech codecs (such as USAC—Unified Speech and Audio Coding) with traditional music codecs into a single architecture.

The encoder dynamically switches coding modes frame-by-frame based on the input signal. This ensures that speech retains maximum intelligibility while music preserves acoustic richness, all at the same low bitrate. Furthermore, integrated loudness and dynamic range control metadata ensure consistent audio volume across different program segments.

Global Standardization and Hardware Availability

For international adoption, DRM requires an open, non-proprietary international standard certified by organizations like ISO/IEC, ETSI, and the ITU. MPEG-4 AAC is universally recognized and widely deployed in consumer silicon, smartphones, automotive infotainment systems, and standalone receivers.

Because decoder implementations are readily available and optimized for low-power DSPs and microcontrollers, manufacturers can produce DRM receivers at lower costs, facilitating the transition from analog to digital broadcasting globally.
