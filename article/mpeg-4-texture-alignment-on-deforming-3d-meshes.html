MPEG-4 Texture Alignment on Deforming 3D Meshes

This article explores how the MPEG-4 standard maintains precise surface texture alignment on dynamically deforming 3D meshes. Through features defined in MPEG-4 Systems (BIFS) and the Animation Framework eXtension (AFX), the standard decouples geometric vertex displacement from 2D parameter coordinates. By binding UV texture coordinates directly to mesh topology and leveraging barycentric interpolation alongside skeletal skinning and morphing nodes, MPEG-4 ensures textures remain locked to surfaces regardless of real-time geometric deformation.

Vertex-Bound Texture Parameterization

MPEG-4 3D graphics represent polygonal models using IndexedFaceSet nodes. In this structure, 2D texture coordinates \((u, v)\) are mapped directly to individual vertex indices rather than absolute 3D world coordinates.

Because the UV coordinates are defined per vertex in the base mesh reference frame, any spatial transformation applied to a vertex automatically carries its corresponding texture anchor point with it. When the 3D position vector \((x, y, z)\) of a vertex changes over time, its assigned \((u, v)\) coordinate remains constant within the local parameter space, preventing the texture from sliding across the surface.

BIFS Interpolators and CoordinateMorpher Nodes

To animate geometric deformation, MPEG-4 utilizes Binary Format for Scenes (BIFS) interpolator nodes, such as CoordinateInterpolator and CoordinateMorpher. These nodes drive mesh deformation by interpolating between predefined sets of 3D vertex positions (keyframe targets).

During morph-target animation, MPEG-4 recalculates vertex positions dynamically:

\[V(t) = \sum_{i} w_i(t) \cdot V_i\]

Where \(V_i\) represents target vertex positions and \(w_i(t)\) represents the interpolation weights. Because the underlying mesh topology, face connectivity, and texture coordinate arrays remain unchanged during this calculation, the texture map stays rigidly aligned to the moving polygonal faces.

MPEG-4 AFX Skeletal Animation and Vertex Blending

MPEG-4 Part 16, known as the Animation Framework eXtension (AFX), introduces advanced deformation techniques such as skeletal skinning and bone-based vertex blending.

In skeletal deformation, each vertex position is computed as a weighted sum of transformations from adjacent bones:

\[V' = \sum_{j} W_j \cdot M_j \cdot V_{bind}\]

Here, \(M_j\) is the transformation matrix for bone \(j\), \(W_j\) is the bone weight, and \(V_{bind}\) is the initial bind pose coordinate. Because skinning transforms the geometric vertices before the rasterization stage, the 2D texture coordinates remain bound to their respective vertices throughout the transformation hierarchy.

Barycentric Rasterization Across Dynamic Triangles

Once deformed vertex coordinates are computed, the hardware rasterizer maps the texture across the interior of each triangular face using barycentric coordinates:

\[P = \lambda_1 V_1 + \lambda_2 V_2 + \lambda_3 V_3\] \[UV_P = \lambda_1 UV_1 + \lambda_2 UV_2 + \lambda_3 UV_3\]

Where \(\lambda_1, \lambda_2, \lambda_3\) represent the barycentric weights (\(\sum \lambda_i = 1\)).

As a triangle stretches, rotates, or compresses due to dynamic mesh deformation, the interior texture mapping scales proportionally with the triangle’s new shape. This perspective-correct barycentric interpolation guarantees that surface textures maintain seamless visual continuity without shearing, tearing, or texture drift across frame updates.
