How MPEG-4 ALS Uses Linear Predictive Coding

This article provides an overview of how MPEG-4 Audio Lossless Coding (ALS) leverages Linear Prediction Coefficients (LPC) to achieve high-efficiency, mathematically lossless audio compression. By analyzing the time-domain correlation of audio signals, ALS estimates future samples from past observations, subtracts these estimates to generate a low-energy residual, and encodes this residual alongside the filter parameters to allow bit-perfect reconstruction.

The Principle of Linear Prediction in ALS

Linear Predictive Coding (LPC) operates on the assumption that an audio sample at time index \(n\), denoted as \(x[n]\), can be modeled as a linear combination of its preceding \(P\) samples:

\[\hat{x}[n] = \sum_{k=1}^{P} a_k \cdot x[n-k]\]

Here, \(P\) represents the prediction order, and \(a_k\) are the linear prediction coefficients.

In MPEG-4 ALS, the encoder computes the predicted sample \(\hat{x}[n]\) and calculates the residual (or error) signal:

\[e[n] = x[n] - \text{round}(\hat{x}[n])\]

Because audio signals typically exhibit strong temporal correlation, the residual signal \(e[n]\) contains significantly lower variance and entropy than the original waveform. Transmitting the small residual values along with the quantized filter coefficients requires fewer bits than transmitting the raw pulse-code modulation (PCM) data.

Coefficient Estimation and Order Selection

MPEG-4 ALS segments the incoming audio stream into frames and sub-blocks. For each block, the encoder calculates the optimal prediction coefficients:

  1. Autocorrelation and Levinson-Durbin: The encoder computes the autocorrelation matrix of the windowed frame. It then applies the Levinson-Durbin recursion algorithm to efficiently solve the Yule-Walker equations, deriving the optimal LPC coefficients that minimize the mean squared error of the residual.
  2. Adaptive Prediction Order: ALS supports a wide range of prediction orders, scaling from low orders (e.g., \(P = 4\) to \(30\)) for simpler material up to extremely high orders (e.g., \(P \le 1023\)) for complex harmonic structures. The encoder evaluates rate-distortion trade-offs to choose the optimal order for each frame.

Coefficient Representation and Stability

Direct quantization of linear prediction coefficients \(a_k\) can cause the prediction filter to become unstable or sensitive to quantization noise. To resolve this, MPEG-4 ALS transforms the LPC coefficients into alternative representations before quantization:

  • Parcor (Partial Correlation) Coefficients: The Levinson-Durbin algorithm naturally produces reflection coefficients (Parcor coefficients). If the magnitude of every reflection coefficient remains strictly less than one (\(|k_i| < 1\)), filter stability is guaranteed.
  • Log Area Ratios (LAR): ALS often converts reflection coefficients into Log Area Ratios or arcsine representations. This non-linear mapping reduces spectral sensitivity to quantization errors, allowing efficient transmission using a limited number of bits per coefficient.

Short-Term and Long-Term Prediction

MPEG-4 ALS extends basic LPC by combining short-term and long-term predictive models:

  • Forward-Adaptive Short-Term LPC: The standard LPC filter captures the spectral envelope and local sample correlations over short durations. The coefficients are calculated once per block and sent explicitly in the bitstream header.
  • Backward-Adaptive Prediction: For enhanced performance without increasing header overhead, ALS can optionally use adaptive algorithms (such as normalized Least Mean Squares) where both the encoder and decoder continuously adjust prediction taps based on reconstructed past samples.
  • Long-Term Prediction (LTP): To model tonal and harmonic components with periods longer than the short-term LPC window, ALS can employ a pitch predictor. LTP identifies repeating periodic structures across hundreds of samples, removing pitch-related redundancy prior to residual entropy coding.

Lossless Reconstruction

At the decoding stage, the process is fully deterministic:

  1. The decoder parses the bitstream to extract and dequantize the LPC parameters, reconstructing the prediction filter.
  2. The decoder reads and entropy-decodes the residual samples \(e[n]\) using Golomb-Rice or arithmetic decoding.
  3. The original sample is reconstructed by adding the integer-rounded prediction to the decoded residual:

\[x[n] = e[n] + \text{round}\left( \sum_{k=1}^{P} a_k \cdot x[n-k] \right)\]

Because the identical rounding and filtering operations are performed at both the encoder and decoder, the restored output matches the original input bit-for-bit.
