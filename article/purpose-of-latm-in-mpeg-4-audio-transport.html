Purpose of LATM in MPEG-4 Audio Transport

This article provides an overview of the Low-overhead Audio Transport Multiplex (LATM) layer defined in the MPEG-4 standard (ISO/IEC 14496-3). It explains LATM’s primary purpose, its operational role in transporting Advanced Audio Coding (AAC) data, and how it balances low protocol overhead with robust stream configuration and synchronization for broadcast and streaming environments.

The primary purpose of the LATM layer in MPEG-4 is to provide a lightweight, efficient transport and multiplexing mechanism for streaming MPEG-4 audio payloads—primarily AAC (Advanced Audio Coding)—over bandwidth-constrained and packet-oriented networks. It bridges the gap between raw audio frames (Access Units) and the underlying delivery channel without imposing the heavy overhead typically associated with generic transport protocols.

LATM accomplishes this through several key technical mechanisms:

1. Minimizing Transmission Overhead

Unlike older encapsulation formats such as ADTS (Audio Data Transport Stream), which attach a complete header to every individual audio frame, LATM minimizes redundant metadata. It separates static stream parameters from dynamic frame data, allowing the multiplexer to send lightweight headers for routine audio frames while preserving critical network bandwidth.

2. Flexible Configuration Transmission (StreamMuxConfig)

LATM carries the StreamMuxConfig data structure, which informs the audio decoder about sample rates, channel configurations, and audio object types. LATM allows this configuration to be transmitted:

  • In-band: Sent periodically within the audio stream to allow decoders to join a live stream dynamically (random access) or handle mid-stream audio format changes.
  • Out-of-band: Transmitted via external signaling protocols (such as SDP in RTSP/SIP setups), entirely eliminating the need to resend configuration data in the audio stream.

3. Synchronization with LOAS

When used in transmission environments without inherent packet framing, LATM is commonly paired with LOAS (Low Overhead Audio Stream). LOAS encapsulates LATM by adding a synchronization word (0x2B7), enabling decoders to quickly detect frame boundaries and achieve lock in continuous bitstreams.

4. Multiplexing Multiple Audio Programs and Layers

LATM can multiplex multiple audio sub-streams into a single bitstream. This enables the transport of scalable audio layers (such as base layers and enhancement layers for variable bitrates) or multilingual audio tracks within a unified transport structure.

Through these functions, the LATM layer ensures that MPEG-4 audio can be efficiently deployed across modern digital broadcasting standards, including DVB, ISDB, and 3GPP mobile streaming environments.
